## Summary

This RFC sets the focus for the coming year. The focus is project health, specifically as it relates to the project's governance structure. The work has three parts. The first is to establish charters for the teams of the Rust project. The second is to provide a unified process and vocabulary across the project. The third is to create a single place for tracking the ongoing projects. The core team will drive these efforts in coordination with all Rust teams and working groups.

## Motivation

As the teams have grown over the years, members have shared fewer teams with one another. Shared membership used to lead to a steady exchange between teams without any effort. That exchange has become thinner. [TODO: give a measure of the change, such as the number of teams or members, or the number of cross-team discussions, or state that the change is qualitative.]

The obstacle is the cost of finding the right people for a new idea. A member who wants to start a project must know which team to consult, and a member who is asked to evaluate an idea must know what other teams are doing. Without shared membership, that knowledge has to be written down. [TODO: give a concrete case from the material where a new idea had to be routed to the wrong team or was duplicated.]

This RFC aims to improve the project's written documentation. The documentation serves two audiences. New members need a smoother onboarding process, and current members need enough information to judge which team to consult on a new idea or project.

## Design

The three parts of the roadmap address the obstacle in different ways.

Team charters state what each team is responsible for and how it makes decisions. A charter lets a member decide whether a question belongs to a team without asking the team first. [TODO: state what a charter must contain, and which teams have agreed to write one.]
