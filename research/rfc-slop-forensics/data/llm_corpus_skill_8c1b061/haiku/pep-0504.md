# PEP 504: Using the System RNG by default

## Summary

Python's module-level functions in the `random` module use the Mersenne Twister, a deterministic generator. Programmers who handle security-sensitive data must know to switch to a cryptographically secure source instead: `os.urandom`, `random.SystemRandom`, or a third-party library such as `cryptography`. ## The problem

The problem is that developers who do not know they are doing security-sensitive work call the default module-level functions anyway. Their users then carry risks that the developers never chose. The material describes this as a chronic problem, not an acute one.

The distinction matters for how the problem is measured. An acute problem produces visible failures soon after it appears. A chronic one builds up while nothing visibly breaks. The material states that the delay between the introduction of a security flaw and its exploitation is often long. Because of that delay, developers do not learn from experience in the usual way: a mistake made today may not show its cost until well after the code has shipped and the author has moved on.

## Why the current guidance is not enough

The material's position is that documentation pointing developers to the secure interfaces has not solved the problem. The abstract says that the developers who need the secure interfaces are often the ones who do not know they need them. Guidance that a reader has to recognize as relevant before it applies cannot help a reader who does not recognize the risk.

The abstract does not claim that the existing documentation is inadequate, and this document does not say so either. The claim concerns what a developer who is unaware of the risk will do, and that is a claim about the default.
