# Rust RFC 3037: 2021 roadmap

## Summary

The focus of this year is project health, specifically as it relates to Rust's governance structure. There are three efforts:

* Establish charters for the teams in the Rust project.
* Provide a unified process and vocabulary across the project.
* Create a single place to track the list of ongoing projects.

The core team will drive these efforts in coordination with all Rust teams and working groups.

## Motivation

Rust has grown, and so has the number of teams that work on it. When the project was smaller, people sat on several teams at once, and information moved between teams as a side effect of that shared membership. The abstract states that, as teams have grown over the years, "there is increasingly less cross-chatter that naturally happens due to shared membership of teams."

Two consequences follow, and both are named in the abstract. A new member has a harder time learning how the project works, because the knowledge sits in people's heads and not in documents. A current member has a harder time deciding whom to consult about a new idea or project, because it is not obvious which team owns a given area.

This RFC proposes to deal with both by improving the written documentation. It is a roadmap and not a technical change to Rust: it sets the focus of the year and does not alter the language, the compiler or the libraries.

### Charters

A team charter states what the team is responsible for. The abstract's own reason for charters is the second consequence above: a person with a new idea needs to find the team that owns the area. A charter that lists scope answers this directly. A charter that lists the team's processes helps the new member.

## Drawbacks

The work takes the time of people who also do technical work. Time spent on charters is not spent elsewhere. The material gives no estimate of the effort, so this document cannot say how large the cost is. [TODO: add an estimate if the author has one.]

## Summary of the decision

The proposal is to spend the year on three governance efforts that serve one need: that members, new and current, can learn how the project works and find the right team quickly. It makes no technical change. The material supports the need only through a statement about reduced cross-chatter, and the evidence for that statement is an open item above.
