# PEP 223: Change the Meaning of `\x` Escapes

## Abstract

This PEP changes `\x` escapes, in both 8-bit and Unicode strings, so that they consume exactly the two hex digits that follow. The PEP treats the current behavior as an original design flaw. The author expects four results: clearer expression in all flavors of string, a cleaner Unicode story, better compatibility with Perl regular expressions, and minimal risk to existing code.

## Specification

A `\x` escape is followed by exactly two hex digits. The two digits give one character value from 0 to 255.

## Rationale

### The central decision

The change alters the meaning of existing syntax. A reader should ask why the PEP does not add a new escape, for example one with a different letter, and leave `\x` alone. A new escape would break nothing. The PEP's answer must appear here. [TODO: the author to give the reason a new escape was rejected. The abstract's phrase "correcting an original design flaw" suggests that the author wants the common spelling to mean the right thing, but this is an inference, and the decision history must come from the author.]

The cost of changing the existing escape is that programs relying on the old meaning change behavior. The cost of adding a new escape is that the flawed one stays, and every new Python programmer learns two. This is the trade-off the PEP exists to resolve, and the current draft does not weigh the two. [TODO: weigh them.]
