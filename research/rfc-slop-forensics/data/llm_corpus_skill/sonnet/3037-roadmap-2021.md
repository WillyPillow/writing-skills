# Rust RFC 3037: Roadmap for 2021

## Summary

The focus of this year is project health, specifically as it relates to Rust's governance structure. The roadmap has three efforts.

* Establish charters for the teams in the Rust project.
* Provide a unified process and vocabulary across the project.
* Create a single place for tracking the list of ongoing projects.

The core team will drive these efforts in coordination with all Rust teams and working groups.

## Motivation

The Rust project is organized in teams and working groups, each with its own scope and way of working. Over the years the number of teams has grown. In the past, much coordination happened without effort, because people belonged to several teams at once, and information passed through shared membership. As teams multiplied, the overlap of memberships shrank, and so did the cross-chatter it produced.

The result, in the words of the abstract, is that there is "increasingly less cross-chatter that naturally happens due to shared membership of teams". Two groups of people feel the effect.

1. A new team member has to learn what the team does, how it makes decisions, and whom it consults. Today the answer is spread across individuals and old threads. A smoother onboarding needs written documentation.
2. A current team member who has a new idea or project has to decide whom to consult. Without a record of what each team covers, the member relies on personal knowledge of the other teams, and the knowledge is shorter than it used to be.

The RFC therefore aims at two outcomes: better written documentation for onboarding, and a better ability for current members to find the people to consult. ### Unified process and vocabulary

At present, teams may use different words for the same step or the same word for different steps, and may follow different procedures for similar decisions. ## Drawbacks

The roadmap asks for time from volunteers. Charters, a shared vocabulary and a tracking list are documents that need writing and maintenance, and the people who write them are the people who would otherwise work on code. [TODO: the author should estimate the cost or state that it is unknown.]

Documents that describe the organization may become out of date. A charter that no longer matches how a team works misleads the new member whom it is meant to help. [TODO: the author should state how charters are kept current, for example by a periodic review.]

A uniform process can fit some teams worse than their own. [TODO: the author should say whether teams can have exceptions.]

## Alternatives

The following are suggestions for the author to evaluate. The draft does not know what the project considered.

- Do not write charters and rely on a directory of teams with a one-line description each. This costs less and tells the reader less.
- Keep the vocabulary per team and add a glossary that maps between them. This keeps team autonomy at the cost of a translation step for every reader.
- Assign the roadmap's items to a new team. This spreads the load, and the abstract's choice of the core team implies a different view.
